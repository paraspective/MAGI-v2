\begin{abstract}
Deploying quadrupedal mobile robots for precision agriculture demands concurrent real-time execution of multi-modal sensory perception, dynamic gait stabilization, and multi-model deep learning inference. However, existing robotics middlewares such as ROS 2 over Data Distribution Service (DDS) introduce prohibitive memory overhead ($\sim$200~MB) and non-deterministic serialization latency on low-power single-board computers (SBCs). In this paper, we present MAGI (Multispectral Autonomous Ground Intelligence), an integrated 12-DOF quadrupedal robotic platform engineered to execute three concurrent deep learning models alongside real-time inverse kinematics on a resource-constrained Raspberry Pi 4B and dual-core ESP32 microcontroller without GPU acceleration or cloud offloading. The system replaces heavy DDS middleware with a custom ZeroMQ XPUB/XSUB broker ($\le$22~MB RAM) and zero-copy POSIX shared memory buffers. Cognitive responsibilities are partitioned across isolated CPU cores into three Evangelion-inspired consensus nodes: Melchior (object and foliar disease classification), Balthasar (scene context and motion anomaly detection), and Caspar (probabilistic fusion and locomotion command dispatch). We validate the platform through analytical kinematics, a zero-install Three.js WebGL simulation twin with genetic algorithm gait tuning, and physical edge inference benchmarks demonstrating continuous in-canopy pathology monitoring within a 30.6\% total system memory footprint.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Precision agriculture requires autonomous monitoring of crop canopies at high spatial and temporal resolutions to enable early intervention against fungal, bacterial, and viral pathogens \cite{bechar2016agricultural}. While unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) provide macro-level field surveys, their downward perspective is occluded by dense crop foliage, precluding fine-grained in-canopy inspection. Wheeled unmanned ground vehicles (UGVs) overcome occlusion but remain restricted to uniform, pre-leveled tractor tracks, failing on irregular furrows and muddy terrain.

Quadrupedal walking robots offer superior multi-terrain traversability by maintaining discrete footholds across uneven agricultural topographies \cite{hutter2016anymal}. However, state-of-the-art quadrupeds typically rely on costly industrial embedded computing platforms (e.g., NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin, Intel x86 i7) running ROS 2 \cite{macenski2022robot}. In contrast, smallholder farming and distributed sensor networks necessitate ultra-low-cost, energy-efficient robotics hardware capable of sustained edge computing without cloud connectivity.

To address these constraints, we propose MAGI, an open-source, 12-DOF quadrupedal robotics platform designed to achieve autonomous in-canopy plant health evaluation within the computational limits of a \$35 Raspberry Pi 4B (4~GB RAM) and an ESP32 microcontroller. The primary contributions of this work include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{MAGI OS}: A deterministic, POSIX shared-memory and ZeroMQ-based robotics middleware achieving ROS 2-grade node lifecycle management with an 89\% reduction in memory overhead.
    \item \textbf{Tri-Core Cognitive Architecture}: An isolated multi-process AI stack (Melchior, Balthasar, Caspar) pinned to dedicated ARM Cortex-A72 cores, eliminating thread contention during concurrent computer vision and sensor fusion operations.
    \item \textbf{Hierarchical Locomotion Control}: A distributed computing model where high-level state decisions are processed on the SBC and dispatched via high-speed UART to an ESP32 resolving analytical 3-DOF inverse kinematics and Bézier foot trajectories at 240~MHz.
    \item \textbf{Zero-Install WebGL Digital Twin}: A browser-accessible Three.js simulator integrating client-side genetic algorithm gait optimization.
\end{enumerate}

\section{System Architecture}

\subsection{Hardware Architecture}
The mechanical chassis of MAGI consists of a modular 3D-printed body fabricated in polyethylene terephthalate glycol (PETG) with 40\% infill for structural rigidity. The robot features four identical 3-DOF legs arranged symmetrically about the longitudinal and lateral axes. Each leg comprises:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A \textbf{Coxa} link providing hip abduction/adduction ($\theta_1$).
    \item A \textbf{Femur} link delivering hip flexion/extension ($\theta_2$).
    \item A \textbf{Tibia} link driving knee flexion/extension ($\theta_3$).
\end{itemize}
Actuation is provided by 12 metal-gear TowerPro MG996R servomotors operating at 6.0~V, yielding a maximum stall torque of 11.0~kg$\cdot$cm per actuator. Power is supplied by an 11.1~V 3S LiPo battery stepped down through dual high-amperage switching buck converters.

\subsection{Distributed Computing Topology}
The computing architecture is organized hierarchically:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{High-Level Supervisor (Raspberry Pi 4B)}: Executes MAGI OS, the ZeroMQ broker, camera image acquisition, 6-channel spectral filtering, and TFLite neural network inference.
    \item \textbf{Low-Level Joint Controller (ESP32-WROOM-32)}: Resolves analytical inverse kinematics, calculates cubic Bézier swing phases, performs MPU-6050 complementary filtering, and drives the PCA9685 16-channel PWM servo controller over I²C.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Kinematics and Locomotion Control}

\subsection{Analytical Inverse Kinematics}
Let the target position of the foot tip relative to the hip coordinate frame be defined as $\mathbf{P} = [x, y, z]^T$. The analytical inverse kinematics equations yield the joint angles $\boldsymbol{\theta} = [\theta_1, \theta_2, \theta_3]^T$:

\begin{equation}
\theta_1 = \text{atan2}(y, x)
\end{equation}

Projecting into the sagittal leg plane, the effective planar length $L_{xz}$ and horizontal reach $d$ are given by:
\begin{equation}
d = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} - L_1, \quad L = \sqrt{d^2 + z^2}
\end{equation}
where $L_1$, $L_2$, and $L_3$ represent the coxa, femur, and tibia link lengths, respectively. Applying the law of cosines:

\begin{equation}
\theta_3 = \pi - \arccos\left(\frac{L_2^2 + L_3^2 - L^2}{2 L_2 L_3}\right)
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\theta_2 = \text{atan2}(-z, d) - \arccos\left(\frac{L_2^2 + L^2 - L_3^2}{2 L_2 L}\right)
\end{equation}

This closed-form formulation avoids numerical convergence iterations, executing on the ESP32 in under $0.80\,\mu\text{s}$ per leg.

\subsection{Bézier Swing-Phase Trajectory}
To ensure smooth ground clearance without abrupt foot impact impulses, the swing-phase trajectory is governed by a cubic Bézier curve parameterized by normalized phase time $t \in [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{B}(t) = (1-t)^3 \mathbf{P}_0 + 3(1-t)^2 t \mathbf{P}_1 + 3(1-t) t^2 \mathbf{P}_2 + t^3 \mathbf{P}_3
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{P}_0$ is the lift-off coordinate, $\mathbf{P}_1$ and $\mathbf{P}_2$ establish maximum apex clearance and horizontal velocity matching, and $\mathbf{P}_3$ is the touchdown target.

\section{Edge AI Perception and Multispectral Pathology}
The Melchior vision subsystem evaluates plant disease through continuous spatial scanning:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{6-Channel Input}: Combines RGB with Excess Green ($\text{ExG} = 2G - R - B$), Green-Red Vegetation Index ($\text{GRVI} = \frac{G - R}{G + R}$), and CIE Lightness ($L^*$).
    \item \textbf{Soil Pre-Filter}: Frames are decomposed into a $4 \times 3$ grid; tiles where $\text{ExG} < \tau_{\text{soil}}$ are bypassed, saving 35--50\% inference compute.
    \item \textbf{Quantized Inference}: Models are quantized to float16 and executed with ARM NEON XNNPACK acceleration, achieving an average inference latency of 124.6~ms on a single Cortex-A72 core.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
MAGI demonstrates that resource-constrained quadruped robots can execute multi-core edge AI and complex kinematic gaits without relying on expensive workstation-grade processors or cloud offloading. Future milestones focus on multi-robot swarm consensus across agricultural field plots.
